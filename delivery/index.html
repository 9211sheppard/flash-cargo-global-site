The final step

Delivered to Your Door.

Delivery is the final step of the sourcing plan: moving the approved goods by air, full or shared ocean containers and truck, with customs and final receiving arrangements coordinated. Cargo Global agrees the route, quote inclusions and handoffs for the actual order. If you already have the goods, choose Delivery only.

Begin with the approved order

Transport planning starts with the goods that have actually been approved. Keep the product description, quantity, value, packing and purchase references together. A delivery request for a packed shipment needs different information from a preliminary sourcing enquiry where production has not begun. Tell us whether the goods are finished, whether an inspection release is outstanding and whether the supplier has confirmed the collection location.

As part of a sourcing order, delivery follows supplier selection, product approval and the agreed quality checks. It is not a replacement for those decisions. If your own supplier is already selected, the Delivery only option collects the origin, destination, packed dimensions, weight and handling requirements without creating a new supplier-search request.

Choose a route around the requirement

Air, ocean and road are options within a buying plan, not promises of a particular arrival date. The useful comparison considers production readiness, shipment size, destination, handling and the point by which the goods are needed. A lighter product can still occupy substantial space, while a dense product can affect handling and equipment choices. Accurate packed measurements are therefore important.

A route is proposed for the actual order and service scope. We do not advertise real-time tracking or invented transit guarantees. Where an estimate is supplied, it should be a range supported by a dated schedule or quotation and should distinguish the transport leg from production, inspection, clearance and final delivery. A published generic range is not an order confirmation.

Air delivery within the plan

Air can be considered where the requirement, cargo characteristics and budget support it. The quote needs to state which collection, airport, terminal, clearance and final-delivery services are included. Confirm the packed dimensions, gross weight, number of pieces and any handling restrictions. If the product contains batteries, liquids or other regulated components, that information must be reviewed before an option is approved.

Do not treat a flight duration as the complete door-to-door timetable. Collection, acceptance, screening, transfers, document review and receiving arrangements can sit outside that narrow measure. Changes to the goods or packing after the quote can also affect the plan. We make the relevant conditions visible before booking rather than promising an unsupported fixed number of days.

Full and shared ocean containers

Ocean delivery may use a full container or shared container space, depending on the order and agreed service. The choice affects packing, consolidation, handling and the destination handoff. A supplier’s factory-ready date is not automatically the date when all goods, documents and quality approvals are ready for dispatch. Those milestones need to be checked together.

Confirm the loading method, package count, dimensions, weights and how the goods are protected through the planned handling. If multiple suppliers contribute to one order, agree where the goods meet and how each contribution is identified. The quote should explain the included origin and destination services and any excluded charges, instead of presenting only a port-to-port figure as a delivered price.

Truck and final delivery

The final leg across North America needs a usable receiving plan. Tell us the city, province or state, country and postal code, together with delivery hours, appointment requirements and the equipment available to unload. Identify restricted access, unusual package sizes and any handling that goes beyond a standard delivery point. A site photograph or clear access description can help explain constraints.

Special handling must be agreed rather than inferred from the phrase door to door. Inside placement, stairs, unpacking, installation, debris removal or an appointment with a particular receiving team are separate requirements where relevant. State exactly what you need so that the proposed service, cost and receiving responsibilities can be checked before the goods leave the supplier.

Customs clearance and product responsibility

Customs coordination depends on accurate product information and an identified responsible importer. The commercial invoice, packing details, classification, origin and values should be consistent with the approved order. Product-specific permits, licences or agency requirements may also apply. A carrier accepting a package does not by itself establish that the product is admissible at the destination.

CBP explains that the importer remains responsible for compliance even when using a broker. Canadian duties and taxes likewise require the correct classification, origin and valuation information, and regulated food may involve separate licensing requirements. We coordinate the agreed handoff with the appropriate providers; the website does not remove the importer’s legal obligations or promise universal clearance.

What the delivered quote must explain

The itemized quote should identify its currency, validity, scope and the costs that have been confirmed. Collection, packing, inspection, transport, clearance, taxes, destination handling, unloading and final delivery should be included only where the quote states that they are. Exclusions are part of the buying decision and deserve the same attention as the displayed total.

Where a shipment-specific cost is not confirmed, it remains quote required rather than zero. Changes to dimensions, weight, quantity, delivery location or receiving requirements should trigger a review. Agree how any additional cost will be approved. An itemized landed-price plan is useful because it makes those decisions explicit, not because a single number can eliminate every possible change.

Release, updates and receiving records

Before dispatch, identify who can authorize release and which inspection, sample or document conditions must be completed. Keep the shipment references tied to the approved purchase and packing records. Status updates should describe the milestone that is actually confirmed. A production update, collection confirmation and delivery appointment are different events and should not be presented as interchangeable.

At receipt, retain the delivery documents and record any visible discrepancy through the agreed process. Do not assume that every claim has the same deadline or remedy. The applicable supplier, transport and order terms should explain how shortages, damage or other concerns are reported. Product inspection, cargo insurance and contract remedies have different roles and need their own agreed scope.

Plan changes before they become surprises

A buyer’s deadline can change, a receiving site can become unavailable or a supplier can propose a different packing arrangement. Share those changes as soon as they are known. Confirm whether the revised requirement affects the route, cost, inspection release or receiving plan. A change is not agreed merely because a message was sent; record the decision and the updated scope.

The delivery request asks for a private reply email so that questions can be resolved without publishing contact details. We reply within one business day. That response starts the review and is not a booking, a guaranteed rate or an arrival promise. If the product still needs to be found and approved, begin with a sourcing request so that delivery is planned as the last step.

Questions before you begin

Can I request delivery without sourcing?

Yes. Choose Delivery only on the contact page and provide the goods, packing, origin, destination and timeline. This creates a delivery-tagged request rather than an instruction to search for suppliers.

Do you offer both full and shared containers?

Both can be considered within the order’s delivery plan. Suitability depends on the actual goods, packing, volume, readiness and destination. The accepted quote identifies the service rather than assuming every order uses the same option.

Is customs included in a door-to-door price?

Only to the extent stated in the quote. Clearance services, duties, taxes, permits and importer responsibilities must be identified. Door to door does not mean that every government charge or receiving service is automatically included.

Can you promise a transit time before reviewing the goods?

No. A useful estimate needs a supported route and date, accurate packing and a clear service scope. Production, inspection, customs and receiving requirements must also be considered; a flight or sailing duration is not the entire delivery.

Do you provide real-time tracking?

No real-time tracking claim is made here. We coordinate status updates for the order using confirmed milestones and available provider information. The agreed service determines which references and updates can be supplied.

What if I need inside delivery or special handling?

Describe that requirement before the quote is approved. Access, stairs, unloading, unpacking, placement and appointments may change the equipment, provider and cost. These services are not inferred from the word delivery.

What can change a confirmed quote?

Changes to the goods, dimensions, weight, collection point, destination or service scope can require review, along with stated conditions in the quote. The written agreement should explain validity, exclusions and how any change is approved.

When will my request be answered?

We reply within one business day through your private reply email. Submitting the form does not create a transport booking or authorize payment; the route, quote and release conditions must first be reviewed and approved.
