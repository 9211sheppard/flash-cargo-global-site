Start with the result you need

A useful sourcing request explains what the product must do, not only what it looks like. Share the intended use, materials, dimensions, finish, tolerances and quantity. Tell us which specifications are essential and which can change if an alternative improves availability or cost. A drawing, an existing sample or a clear photo reference helps narrow the search, but any specification that affects acceptance should also be written down.

Include the destination country and city, the delivery deadline or a flexible time window, and a target price in USD or CAD where you have one. An origin preference is optional. If your product has a brand, design or licensed feature, explain your rights to use it. Do not include passwords, payment-card details or confidential files unrelated to the request.

What we source

Buyers approach us with industrial parts, packaging, furniture and fixtures, apparel and textiles, electronics, food ingredients and building materials. Each category needs a different brief. A replacement part may depend on fit and material grade; packaging can depend on print accuracy and contact suitability; furniture needs finish samples and delivery-access planning. We evaluate the actual requirement rather than treating products with similar photographs as interchangeable.

Availability is reviewed before an order is accepted. The invitation to ask about any product is not an offer to obtain prohibited, counterfeit or unsuitable goods. A regulated item may require testing, permits or registration before a purchase can proceed. We discuss those checks early so that an attractive supplier quote does not become an unusable order.

Packaging

Include the material, pack dimensions, printing, artwork revision and intended use in your brief. Identify whether packaging touches food or another regulated product, and what protection it must provide. A sample and written artwork approval can be agreed before production. Minimums and any setup charges remain quote required until the supplier confirms them.

Building materials

Specify the material grade, measurements, finish, quantity and project destination. Identify required standards and supporting test documents before approving a supplier. Samples should represent the proposed finish and construction. Review delivery access, handling equipment and unloading requirements alongside the order so that the product and receiving plan fit together.

How we curate the supplier shortlist

We curate a shortlist through our global supplier network, reviewing the business and capability relevant to your requirements. That review can include the company identity, production location, product experience, document consistency, sample availability and ability to meet the agreed specification. A business selling a broad catalogue is not automatically the manufacturer of every item it offers; the proposed supply chain needs to be understood.

Vetting is a review process, not a promise that every future unit will be perfect. The checks, evidence and any remaining questions should be recorded before an introduction or approval. A certificate must match the product, issuing body, site and scope being considered. We do not replace a product-specific review with a badge, a marketplace rating or an unverified claim.

Compare like-for-like supplier quotes

A low unit price is meaningful only when the offers cover the same product and conditions. Compare the material, grade, finish, quantity unit, packaging, tooling, sample charges and production scope. Confirm whether the offer relates to an existing item or a new production run. An excluded mould, print setup or test can change the buying decision even where the headline price looks attractive.

We make the relevant differences visible rather than silently choosing the lowest figure. The supplier shortlist and its timing are confirmed after reviewing the brief. If a cost has not been established, it remains quote required. A blank figure is not treated as zero, and a promotional price is not carried into your order without confirming its conditions.

Samples and written approval

Samples can be arranged when they help confirm the product before purchase. Agree which version the sample represents, the sample quantity, any charge and the approval criteria. For a custom product, identify the drawing, artwork or specification revision that should control the order. A photograph can support a discussion, but it does not replace a physical sample where texture, fit, strength or finish is important.

Record the approval and any accepted differences. If a later production change is proposed, review it against the approved version instead of assuming it is equivalent. Sample approval should also state what has not been tested. Passing a visual check is not the same as proving durability, electrical safety, food suitability or compliance with a destination requirement.

Inspection before shipment

Where agreed, we facilitate a pre-shipment inspection by a local independent inspector. The scope should identify the product, sampling approach, checks, acceptance criteria and report expected. The inspection might address appearance, measurements, quantity, packing or agreed functional checks. Specialist tests need to be named and arranged separately where they fall outside a general inspection.

Agree who receives the report and who can authorize release. If a concern is found, decide whether the goods must be corrected, rechecked, replaced or held before dispatch. Inspection fees, reinspection and the effect on timing should be addressed in the written plan. An inspection reduces specific risks; it cannot guarantee the absence of every defect.

Payment protection through clear approvals

The first protection is knowing exactly what you are authorizing. Review the supplier option, specification, price, payment stages and order terms before funds are committed. Confirm how the payee relates to the supplier and investigate unexpected changes through the agreed contact route. Keep the purchase approval separate from an informal sample discussion or a delivery enquiry.

Cargo Global does not claim a blanket escrow service, automatic refund or universal buyer-protection policy. Any deposit, balance, cancellation, defect, warranty or claim arrangement must be agreed for the actual order. Do not send banking credentials or card details through the request form. Submitting a brief does not place an order or authorize a charge.

Minimum orders and the landed-price plan

Minimum order quantity: quote required. The minimum can depend on the product, colour, size mix, production method or packaging. Ask whether a supplier permits a mixed order and whether a smaller trial run changes the price. A sample quantity and a production minimum are different decisions, and neither should be inferred from a photograph or catalogue headline.

Before approval, the quote identifies its currency, validity, product price and the agreed sample, inspection, customs and delivery inclusions. Exclusions need the same attention as the total. Confirm who handles destination taxes, unloading and appointments, and whether an estimate can change. One coordinated plan should make those responsibilities easier to see, not conceal them behind a single unexplained number.

Delivery is the final buying step

The route follows the product and delivery requirement. Air, full or shared ocean containers and truck can be considered as appropriate, with customs clearance and final delivery coordinated in the agreed scope. Production completion, inspection release, export documentation and receiving arrangements must align before dispatch. We do not publish unsupported transit promises or claim that a live map guarantees arrival.

At the destination, confirm the receiving hours, appointment rules, unloading equipment and any restricted access. Keep the approved specification and commercial documents together with the inspection and delivery records. If you already have the goods and only need this final step, choose Delivery only on the contact page rather than creating a supplier-search request.

Questions before you begin

Do I need a finished specification?

No, but distinguish your requirements from open decisions. Share the intended use and any reference material. We can identify questions that must be settled before a supplier quote or purchase approval becomes meaningful.

Can I request a small order?

Yes. Tell us the quantity and unit. The supplier minimum is quote required and may vary with customisation, packaging or product mix. An application does not promise acceptance of a particular order size.

Can you compare more than one supplier?

We review relevant options against the brief and make material differences visible. The available shortlist and timing depend on the product and evidence. We do not promise a fixed number of suppliers before reviewing the request.

Are samples included in every quote?

Only where the quote says so. Sample production, courier delivery, tooling and specialist testing may carry separate costs. Confirm the sample version and acceptance criteria before treating it as the approved production reference.

Does an inspection guarantee compliance?

No. The inspection covers the agreed scope and cannot establish every product attribute or legal requirement. Identify required tests and documentation before ordering, and agree how failed checks and corrective work will be handled.

Is payment automatically protected by escrow?

No blanket escrow or refund arrangement is advertised here. The actual payment method, approval steps and contractual protections must be agreed before purchase. A request itself does not authorize payment.

Which destinations do you serve?

Our buyer service is directed to the US, Canada and Mexico. Tell us the destination city and any receiving restrictions. Product admissibility and the actual delivery scope are checked for the proposed order.

What happens after I submit?

Your request is stored privately for review and we reply within one business day using your private reply email. The next step is clarifying the brief, not automatically purchasing goods or charging a fee.
